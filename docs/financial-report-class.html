Campaign economics

All amounts are incremental to ordinary store sales. A successful activation generates €2,500 in incremental sales; every activation costs €1,500. Net campaign value is incremental sales less activation spend, before product costs or other overhead.

What was actually observed?

In 2026, 108 of 170 activations succeeded. Applying the forest’s held-out decisions retrospectively would have funded 108 stores: 101 observed successes and 7 failures, for €90,500 retrospective net (€252,500 sales less €162,000 spend). The campaign actually earned €15,000 net; the €75,500 difference is a historical backtest (tree benchmark: €88,000).

For 2027, the 108 repeat stores contribute €77,525.00 expected net at the forest’s probabilities; the 168 selected new stores contribute €104,725.00 expected net. Both are forecasts. Together they make up the €182,250.00 scenario.

Oral-B Italy · Decision and reconciliation

How much should we commit?

Agree the pilot budget before selecting stores. Each pair of activations, one in each test arm, costs €3,000. Choose a cap that can fund the minimum sample needed to make an expansion decision.

Downside if scores are too optimistic

The full commitment needs 166 successful activations out of 276 to cover its cost: 165 successes would lose €1,500; 166 would earn €1,000. The model implies about 239 successes. The 170 historic activations were selected, and neither planned geographic holdout had sufficient support to validate transfer.

Decision and next steps

  1. Team 4 — reconcile the handoff. Verify the workflow fingerprint below, run it into an empty output folder, and reconcile the 276 exact IDs and the three differences on page 1. Use the class exports for these figures; the earlier 287-store tree release and the 278-store RC3 summary describe different policies.
  2. Campaign owner — design the test. Set the spend cap, duration, outcome window, minimum worthwhile return and sample size before seeing results. Freeze the forest and the strict funding rule (more than 60 of 100 votes). Within region and new/repeat groups, randomly assign comparable store pools to forest targeting or current practice and give each arm the same activation budget.
  3. Expansion decision. Track actual activations, successes, spend, and realized net per euro by arm and region. Expand only if the pilot clears the agreed financial criterion with adequate evidence; otherwise hold spending and review where forecasts failed.

Calculation and source record

Score all 480 stores once: 170 historical stores use the forest’s held-out scores; 310 new stores use the forest refit on the historical sample. Each score is the exact vote share (success votes ÷ 100). Fund a new store only if more than 60 of 100 trees vote success (p > 0.60, a 60-vote tie is not funded); a historical store is funded if and only if it succeeded in 2026 (team decision B). Expected euros always use the forest probability. For any selected set: sales = €2,500 × sum(p); spend = €1,500 × count. For equal spend rank positive-value stores by probability descending, then text store ID ascending. Compare the top 170 with all 170 historical IDs, using the same probability wherever a store recurs. Differences use unrounded values; round only for display.
