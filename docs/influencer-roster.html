Influencer Shortlist: Oral-B iO · Evidence and sources

Where each number comes from

Followers: Instagram account totals, from the linked Modash entries for eight creators. A 300,000-follower threshold was a scouting preference, not a condition for joining the roster.

Why Federica wins the first brief

For an Italy-wide introduction, choose the largest estimated Italian Instagram follower base with a demonstrated routine format. Multiply account followers by Modash’s audience-location share for Italy. On the 11 Nov snapshots, Federica: 805k × 95.61% ≈ 770k; Dadda: 752.4k × 94.89% ≈ 714k. Every other creator has fewer than 446k Instagram followers in total. The ranking therefore does not depend on estimating Coco’s Italian share. These are estimated followers, not unique viewers, TikTok audience or buyers.

Federica has executed both parts of the brief: a TikTok get-ready-with-me routine and an Instagram beauty-product feature mentioning Rare Beauty and Sephora Italia (3 Apr 2026). Dadda leads on average plays (955.4k vs 613.6k); Aisha leads on ER (10.28% vs 2.07%). Neither metric measures Italian orders. Comparable fees and iO sales are unavailable, so the pilot tests conversion before buying a second wave.

Coco’s 254,032 Instagram count is from the 24 Oct snapshot; her public TikTok profile lists about 620k separately.

Engagement rate: Modash’s methodology (21 Oct 2026) defines Instagram ER as median likes divided by followers, expressed as a percentage, using roughly the last three months of content. Its TikTok rate uses views as the denominator. The two rates are not interchangeable.

Plays per follower: average Instagram Reel plays ÷ Instagram followers, using the displayed counts. For Dadda, 955.4k ÷ 752.4k = 1.27. For Vincenzo, 368.9k ÷ 374.3k = 0.99. A person can play a Reel more than once: this ratio does not measure unique people reached.

Famiglia Calò: the 25 Nov Instagram entry gives 1.25% ER. Their TikTok account is @famigliacalo. We do not use a TikTok follower count as the denominator for an Instagram rate.

Why try a routine video?

On 26 Nov we checked three featured Instagram captions for each Italy-labeled profile in the Modash skincare list. Five of 57 captions contained “routine,” including hashtags. One was Elisa’s “Hair routine”; the other four used the word in hashtags. The dated tally and post links show all 20 profiles; one Swiss profile was excluded. This supports testing a familiar format, not a claim that toothbrush demand is rising.

There is a commercial reason to try short video too: TikTok Shop Italy’s first-year report (27 Apr 2026) attributes 38% of its turnover since launch to shoppable videos and 20% to LIVE. These are shares of TikTok Shop turnover, not predicted Oral-B sales.

Before booking, ask each creator to confirm current audience location and native account insights. Do not add Instagram and TikTok audiences or treat unverified estimates of fake followers as facts about a creator.

Influencer Shortlist: Oral-B iO · Activation and measurement

The trend we create:
#iOSmileUnlocked / #SorrisoSbloccato

These are our proposed campaign hashtags. Federica introduces the brush in a get-ready-with-me video for TikTok and Instagram. Every activated creator makes one short video for both platforms, adapting the hook to their own audience.

How it works

  1. Hook: the creator films a messy morning face with the text “not unlocked yet.”
  2. Brushing: fast-cut a two-minute routine to music. Show the green pressure cue only on a model that supports it (Oral-B’s iO model guide).
  3. Reveal: where the featured model displays a smiley, frame it beside the creator’s smile. Otherwise use the creator’s smile and caption: “Smile unlocked” / “Sorriso sbloccato.”

Creative freedom: give creators the idea, not a full script. A recognisable reveal links the posts while leaving each creator room for their own style.

How to launch the pilot

  1. Before booking: Team 1 requests Federica’s dated native Italian audience shares on both platforms, her fee, and the median 14-day views and shares of her last five comparable organic videos. If the native figures overturn the geographic advantage or the quote fails the retailer’s budget, revisit the choice before signing.
  2. Before posting: Team 4 and the retailer choose the featured iO SKU, landing page, available stock and contribution per order. Agree a spend cap, minimum tagged-session count and target cost per order before seeing results; use the retailer’s comparable launches and unit margin. Confirm that a test click and test order appear with the right creator, platform and product.
  3. Day 1 — Federica opens: publish her morning-routine video as an Instagram Reel and TikTok, with the same offer and call to action. Give each platform its own tagged bio link; add an Instagram Story link if available and label that placement separately. Record post URLs, timing, costs and any paid boosts. Keep paid and organic results separate.
  4. Day 2 — tracking check: Team 4 checks native views against incoming tagged sessions and verifies checkout events. Fix a broken link before spending more; do not interpret a tracking fault as weak demand.
  5. Day 15 — decide the next wave: use the rule on page 5. If it passes, test Aisha for beauty (highest measured ER, 10.28%) or Dadda for fitness (955.4k average Reel plays), using the same offer and window. For a parent-led iO Kids brief, test Famiglia Calò rather than treating adult iO orders as evidence about children’s products. Keep the remaining creators in reserve.

What to measure before booking the next creator

One row per creator, platform and placement, starting with Federica. Read each post after its own first 14 days. The window and thresholds are pilot design choices, not industry benchmarks. Record raw counts as well as rates. Do not add Instagram and TikTok audiences together.

QuestionMeasure and source
Did people watch?Report native video views on both platforms and Instagram accounts reached where available. Compare views with the creator’s own 14-day median for the five comparable posts; mark a missing baseline unavailable. Views include repeat exposure. Sources: Meta Insights and TikTok Studio.
Did they share?Shares ÷ video views × 100, per post and platform. Compare with the median share rate of those same five posts. Pull both counts from the creator’s native insights above. This campaign KPI is different from Modash’s historical likes/follower ER.
Did people visit?Count iO product-page sessions arriving through each creator/platform link. Use session campaign and source, with a distinct creator ID in utm_content. Google Analytics: campaign URLs.
Did visitors order?Completed iO orders in those tagged landing sessions ÷ tagged landing sessions. Count distinct order IDs containing an iO SKU in the same session and window. Publish orders, sessions and the ratio. Google Analytics: purchase event, items and transaction ID.
Was it affordable?Creator fee + production + paid media, divided by deduplicated attributed iO orders, gives cost per attributed order. Use invoices and the retailer’s order export. Report shared fees once at creator level unless a split was agreed before launch.

Day 15 decision · Team 4 and retailer

Expand the test only if tracking is valid, the agreed session minimum is met, and cost per attributed order is at or below the pre-agreed ceiling (no higher than the retailer’s net contribution per iO order before campaign costs). Revise the hook if views or share rate lag Federica’s own baseline; check offer, stock and checkout if visits arrive but orders do not. Hold if volume or attribution is insufficient; stop paid expansion if measured costs exceed the ceiling. No numerical target is asserted without fee, margin and baseline data.

Zero denominator: show counts and “unavailable,” never a zero rate. With no retailer order data, report attention and visits only. These are attributed orders, not incremental sales; a profitable attribution readout justifies another test, not a national rollout.
