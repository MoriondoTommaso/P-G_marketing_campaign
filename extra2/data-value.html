Where the free data is thin

Each dot is one of the 480 stores, placed by its own coordinates. Pick a layer to size the dots by what OpenStreetMap finds within 1 km (snapshot of 1 January 2026, before the 2026 campaign).

Red bars: too thin to use. Most stores show zero dentists or drugstores, and a zero there usually means "not mapped", not "none nearby". Pharmacies, schools and supermarkets are usable, so only those three went into the test.

The test: same forest, same folds, plus 3 OSM counts

Built in the final KNIME workflow (bottom row): CSV Reader → Joiner on store code (input order kept, so every store stays in its fold) → Column Filter → the forest's own X-Partitioner, Random Forest Learner and Predictor, X-Aggregator, more-than-60-votes rule and Scorer, copied unchanged.

Verdict: drop OpenStreetMap. The keep rule set before the test asks for a higher pooled net and a gain in at least 3 of 5 folds. Net fell from €90,500 to €88,000: one more failure funded and one more success missed. The counts sharpen the ranking a little (AUC 0.932 vs 0.926) but not the funding decisions, which is what the money depends on. On the tree benchmark they changed nothing (€88,000 both times). Retrospective result on the 170 stores activated in 2026.

How we test any new source before paying

  1. Freeze the reference. The chosen forest on its 5 folds: €90,500 on the 170 stores of 2026.
  2. Add dated columns only. Every new value must be measured before the 2026 campaign, or it could leak the outcome.
  3. Rerun the same folds. Same model, same stores in each fold: only the data changes.
  4. Keep it only if it pays. Higher pooled net and a gain in at least 3 of 5 folds. OpenStreetMap failed this test.

What this tells P&G. Area data cannot separate these stores: all 32 area traits and the OpenStreetMap counts were tried. For 12 of the 14, the nearest activated store had the opposite result, and 11 of the 14 are also missed by the tree benchmark, so switching model does not reach them. That points to a local factor we do not observe, such as the retailer, the field team or the store's own sales. Check P&G's internal data first (free), then a free sample of the sales panel before buying. A 2027 pilot that includes northern stores far from a dentist but with many schools would add the labels the models lack. Small groups: read as leads, not proof.

7 successes we skip → store-level demand

Northern stores far from a dentist that still sold. Their own oral care sales (panel, #7) or a field audit (#10) could show the demand the area data hides. Hypothesis, to test.

7 failures we fund → competition and execution

Stores very close to a dentist in bigger towns that did not respond. Competing brands on the shelf and displays (audits, #10) or shopper traffic (footfall, #8) could explain it. Hypothesis, to test.
